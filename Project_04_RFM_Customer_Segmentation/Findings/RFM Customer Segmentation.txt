# RFM Customer Segmentation | Business Findings

## 1. Project Objective

The objective of this project is to segment customers using Recency,
Frequency and Monetary (RFM) analysis and identify customer groups
that require different retention strategies.

---

## 2. Dataset & Data Cleaning

- Original transactions: 1,067,371
- Clean transactions used for RFM: 805,549
- Unique invoices: 53,628
- Unique customers in raw data: 5,942
- Transaction period: 2009-12-01 to 2011-12-09

### Cleaning Rules

The following records were excluded:

- Cancelled invoices
- Quantity <= 0
- Price <= 0
- Missing Customer_ID

The original dataset was preserved and a separate `RFM_Clean`
table was created for analysis.

---

## 3. RFM Methodology

### Recency

Number of days since the customer's most recent purchase.

### Frequency

Number of distinct invoices generated by the customer.

### Monetary

Total customer spending calculated as:

`Quantity × Price`

Each dimension was scored from 1 to 5 using `NTILE(5)`.

- Higher Recency score = more recent purchase
- Higher Frequency score = more purchases
- Higher Monetary score = higher spending

The three scores were combined into an RFM code such as `555`, `451`
or `121`.

## 5. Key Findings

### Champions

1,346 customers were classified as Champions.

This segment contributes 68.87% of the calculated revenue share,
making customer retention an important business focus.

### Potential Loyalists

755 customers fall into the Potential Loyalists segment and contribute
11.66% of revenue share.

These customers can be targeted with recommendations and loyalty
incentives to encourage repeat purchases.

### At Risk

203 customers are classified as At Risk and contribute 5.47% of
revenue share.

This segment represents customers with previous purchasing activity
who may require targeted win-back campaigns.

### Lost Customers

Lost Customers are the largest segment by customer count with
1,782 customers.

However, their revenue share is 3.30%.

This indicates that customer count and revenue contribution can tell
different stories, which is one reason RFM segmentation is useful.

## 6. Recommended Actions

| Segment | Recommended Action |
|---|---|
| Champions | VIP rewards, early access and loyalty benefits |
| Loyal Customers | Loyalty rewards and personalized cross-selling |
| Potential Loyalists | Product recommendations and loyalty incentives |
| New Customers | Welcome offers and second-purchase campaigns |
| At Risk | Targeted win-back campaigns |
| Needs Attention | Re-engagement campaigns |
| Hibernating | Low-cost reactivation campaigns |
| Lost Customers | Final win-back campaign with controlled marketing spend |

## 7. Technical Implementation

The project was completed using SQL Server.

Key SQL techniques:

- Data cleaning
- `TRY_CONVERT`
- `DATEDIFF`
- `COUNT(DISTINCT)`
- `CASE WHEN`
- `NTILE(5)`
- Window functions
- Aggregations
- Revenue-share calculation
- SQL Views

Reusable view created:

`dbo.vw_RFM_Customer_Segmentation`

## 8. Power BI Dashboard

A one-page Power BI dashboard was created to visualize:

- Total Customers
- Total Revenue
- Average Customer Revenue
- At Risk Customers
- Customer Count by Segment
- Revenue by Customer Segment
- Revenue Share by Segment

Power BI was used for visualization, while the RFM calculations and
segmentation logic were performed entirely in SQL.

---

## 9. Business Takeaway

RFM analysis converts transaction-level data into actionable
customer segments.

The analysis shows that different customer groups require different
retention approaches. High-value customers can be prioritized for
retention, while At Risk, Needs Attention and inactive segments can
be targeted with re-engagement campaigns.
